About ATPBR

Version 0.1 · Chapter 1 of 14 · Updated 19 Nov 2026

What is ATPBR?#

The Brazilian Population Tumor Atlas (ATPBR) is a national initiative dedicated to mapping the molecular characteristics of cancer in the Brazilian population and to sharing these data responsibly. Funded by the Ministry of Health in partnership with CNPq (National Council for Scientific and Technological Development) since December 2023, and led by the Brazilian National Cancer Institute (INCA) and the Brazilian Cancer Genome Consortium, the project gathers, organizes and makes available clinical and multi-omics data (genomics, transcriptomics and epigenomics) from cancer patients in different regions of the country.

In practice, ATPBR works as a centralized and secure infrastructure that integrates clinical metadata and molecular results, so that tumor omics data produced in different studies, institutions and technologies can be integrated and compared consistently.

The problem ATPBR helps to solve#

Most of the genomic data that guide precision medicine today were generated in Northern Hemisphere populations.

  • In one of the world's largest repositories, The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), 77% of the samples analyzed came from white patients and only 3% from Hispanic/Latino patients (Spratt et al., 2016).
  • PCAWG/ICGC-TCGA: 76.2% of individuals had European ancestry and only 1.0% were classified as Admixed American/Amerindian/Latino (Campbell et al., 2020).
  • AACR Project GENIE: 72% of patients were classified as white; Hispanic patients had only 57% of the expected representation relative to the US cancer population (Pugh et al., 2022; Cheung et al., 2023).
  • NCI Patient-Derived Models Repository: 75% of the samples are of European ancestry and less than 1% of Native/Latin American ancestry (Molina-Aguilar and Robles-Espinoza, 2023).

These data reveal a persistent gap in the representation of Latin American populations in the main genomic resources used for biomarker discovery, the development of predictive models and the implementation of precision medicine.

This is a problem because the genetic characteristics of one population do not automatically apply to another: variants associated with risk, prognosis and treatment response may differ. Clinical decisions based on poorly representative data tend to be less effective for Brazilians.

Why does Brazilian diversity matter?#

The Brazilian population has great genetic diversity, the result of a demographic history marked by Indigenous, African, European and other ancestral contributions. This diversity represents a scientific opportunity that is still little explored:

  • A study of 2,723 Brazilian genomes identified more than 8.7 million previously undocumented genetic variants (Nunes et al., 2025).
  • The eligibility criteria for genetic testing evaluated in a Brazilian study would fail to identify between 17% and 25% of carriers of pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants associated with hereditary cancer predisposition syndromes (de Oliveira et al., 2022).
  • Some pathogenic germline variants associated with hereditary cancer are more frequent in the Brazilian population than in international reference databases (Boroni et al., 2026).
  • The Brazilian Genome Map Oncology Subproject (BGMO) demonstrated the feasibility of generating tumor-germline WGS in a multicenter setting in Brazil, with 275 patients recruited in the country's five regions and a population composition distinct from that of the large international databases (Ferreira et al., 2026).

These data show that the challenge is not only to generate more Brazilian genomic data, but to ensure that they can be standardized, integrated, governed, shared and reused. ATPBR was designed to fill this gap, creating a national infrastructure capable of turning data that are currently scattered into an integrated resource for research and precision medicine.

Our mission#

To increase the representation of the Brazilian population in cancer research, providing resources that contribute to biomarker discovery, the development of new diagnostic and treatment strategies and, ultimately, the delivery of precision medicine in SUS, Brazil's Unified Health System.

Who is ATPBR for?#

The platform was developed to serve different user profiles, including:

  • Researchers interested in accessing and analyzing cancer data.
  • Institutions and researchers who wish to share data in a standardized and secure way.
  • Bioinformaticians and data scientists looking for data for their multi-omics analyses.
  • Project managers and coordinators who need to follow the data life cycle.
  • Collaborators involved in national cancer research initiatives.

The Start here chapter points out the best reading path for each profile.

What does ATPBR offer?#

ATPBR brings together, in a single environment, resources that cover the entire data life cycle. Each of them is detailed in a chapter of this documentation:

  • Secure storage: national infrastructure for storing and managing clinical and molecular data, with protection and access-control mechanisms (Platform architecture).
  • Data governance and protection: policies that set out responsibilities, access criteria and conditions of use, ensuring the protection of participants and compliance with ethical and legal standards (Data governance).
  • Standardized submission: mechanisms for registering projects and for the structured submission of data from different institutions and studies (Data submission).
  • Integration and harmonization: standardization of clinical, sociodemographic and multi-omics data, enabling the integration and comparison of information produced by different technologies and institutions (Data model and Data dictionary).
  • Reproducible processing and analysis: use of standardized, automated and versioned bioinformatics pipelines, ensuring the consistency, traceability and reproducibility of analyses (Analysis platform).
  • User-friendly, interactive exploration and visualization: tools to query, explore and compare molecular and clinical data, making it easier to identify patterns and generate new scientific hypotheses (Results browser).
  • National sovereignty and responsible sharing: data kept under Brazilian governance, with public access to non-sensitive information and controlled-access mechanisms for sensitive data, respecting usage authorizations, consents and current legislation (Data access).

Commitment to quality and trust#

To ensure reliable and reusable data, ATPBR adopts the FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable) and guides its development by the international standards of the Global Alliance for Genomics and Health (GA4GH), which promote the interoperability, governance and responsible sharing of genomic and clinical data. The platform sets three minimum criteria for including samples: (i) high-quality sequencing data, (ii) informed consent from participants/patients (or a waiver approved by the competent ethics body), in compliance with current ethical and regulatory standards, and (iii) a minimum set of clinical and sociodemographic metadata.

In compliance with the Brazilian General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Law No. 13,709/2018), ATPBR receives only data that have been de-identified beforehand by the participating institutions. The platform does not collect direct identifiers, nor certain variables that could increase the risk of participant re-identification, and the keys that link the codes to individuals remain at the institution of origin. Because the records of the same participant remain linked to one another, these data are pseudonymized, not anonymous: for this reason, they receive the protections that the LGPD provides for sensitive personal data. Data are stored and processed following information security and data governance practices appropriate to scientific research. These principles are revisited throughout the documentation and detailed in Data governance and Usage policies.

In this way, ATPBR seeks to reconcile the protection of participants' privacy with the promotion of open, transparent and reproducible scientific research.
