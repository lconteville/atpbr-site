Platform architecture

Chapter 3 of 14 · Updated 18 Nov 2026

The ATPBR architecture was designed to support the entire life cycle of cancer data, from submission to analysis and the release of results to the scientific community. The platform consists of interoperable modules that work together to ensure traceability, standardization, scalability and reproducibility.

This chapter presents the big picture. Each stage described here is detailed in a chapter of its own, indicated throughout the text.

Architecture overview#

In simplified form, the data flow in ATPBR follows these stages:

Submission → Secure storage → Harmonization → Reproducible analyses → Visualization → Controlled access → Governance

At every stage, the participant-protection principles presented in About ATPBR are applied through role- and permission-based access control, activity logging and auditing, and the management of access requests for controlled-access data. The rules for each stage, and who is responsible for it, are set out in Data governance.

Data ingestion#

Ingestion is the receipt of the metadata and sequencing files submitted by users. The step-by-step process is described in Data submission. Data are organized according to the Data model and receive standardized identifiers, described in Identification and barcode system.

Validation#

After ingestion, data undergo automatic and manual checks of integrity, format, required fields and consistency between metadata and files, as well as a review of the ethics documentation and usage permissions. Being accepted by the system is not the same as being admitted: while there are pending issues, the dataset remains blocked from release and from new uses. The checks and how to correct errors are described in Data submission, and the admission rules in Data governance.

Data storage#

The architecture separates storage into two complementary layers, linked by a metadata catalog:

  • Raw data layer: holds the large-volume files (the submitted FASTQ and the CRAM generated during processing), preserving the originals for traceability and future reanalysis.
  • Structured data layer: holds the data processed and organized for querying, such as harmonized clinical data, genomic alterations and gene expression matrices.
  • Metadata and cataloging: the catalog records projects, investigations, studies, patients, samples, assays, experiments, files and the processing runs performed. It is what makes it possible to locate a dataset, understand its origin and reconstruct the complete history of how the information was generated.

Data processing#

Validated FASTQ files are processed by standardized pipelines, which identify somatic variants and quantify gene expression. The pipelines, their versions and the files generated are described in Analysis platform.

Harmonization and integration#

Processing results are standardized, annotated and integrated with clinical data, which makes it possible to compare different studies. Non-sensitive harmonized data are loaded into the ATPBR cBioPortal.

Exploration and sharing#

Results can be explored in the Results browser (ATPBR cBioPortal). The projects' studies and datasets can be consulted in the platform's Data catalog, provided that their release has been authorized by those responsible for them. To learn more about the release criteria, the access levels (open or controlled) and the procedures for requesting data access, see the Data access section.
