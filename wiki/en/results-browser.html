Results browser

Version 0.1 · Chapter 12 of 14 · Updated 19 Nov 2026

The results generated by the Analysis platform reach users through the ATPBR cBioPortal, an interactive environment for browsing, visualizing and analyzing cancer genomic and clinical data without requiring advanced bioinformatics knowledge. This chapter describes what is available in the portal and how to explore it.

ATPBR uses cBioPortal for Cancer Genomics, version 7.2, an open-source platform widely used by the international scientific community for the integrated exploration of molecular and clinical cancer data.

The portal is aimed at translational researchers, molecular biologists, clinicians, bioinformaticians and data scientists interested in the molecular landscape of Brazilian tumors.

1. Available data#

cBioPortal brings together, in a single study, the data layers generated along the ATPBR workflow:

Layer Source What it lets you explore
Somatic mutations Variants filtered and annotated by nf-core/sarek (WGS, WES, gene panel) Recurrently altered genes, mutation types and the distribution of alterations across cases.
Copy number Segmented copy-number data, when available Genomic gains and losses, broad or focal.
Gene expression TPM matrices from nf-core/rnaseq (RNA-seq) Expression differences between genes, samples and groups, and the transcriptional effect of genomic alterations.
Clinical and sample data Metadata registered at submission (see Data dictionary) Cohort filtering, stratification of analyses and clinical interpretation of findings.

Not every study will have all of these layers: availability depends on the assays performed, the quality of the samples and the release stage. Check the description of each study in the portal.

2. How to navigate#

The typical exploration path has four steps:

  1. Choose a study from the list of studies. Each study corresponds to a curated dataset, usually one tumor type.
  2. Get to know the cohort in the Study View, which summarizes the number of patients and samples, the available data types and the distribution of the main clinical and molecular variables.
  3. Filter the cohort by the available attributes, such as tumor type, histology, sample type, staging or sequencing availability.
  4. Query genes of interest and examine the results in the visualizations described below.

The studies and profiles you can see vary according to each user's permissions and the release stage of the data (see Data access).

3. Main features#

Feature What it is for
Study View Overview of the study and starting point for applying filters to the cohort.
Query By Gene Search for one or more genes in the selected cohort and see how they are altered. By default, it includes non-synonymous mutations, fusions, amplifications and deep deletions.
OncoPrint Sample-by-sample visual summary of the alterations in the queried genes. Helps identify recurrent events, co-occurring and mutually exclusive alterations.
Mutations tab Details of each variant: type, position in the protein and affected samples. Useful for identifying hotspots.
Copy number and expression Explore amplifications, deletions and expression levels, when these data exist in the study.
Plots tab Compare molecular measurements with clinical annotations, for example the expression of a gene by tumor subtype.
Group Comparison Compare molecular and clinical characteristics between user-defined subcohorts.
Survival analysis Outcome plots, when the study includes vital status and follow-up time.
Patient View / Sample View All clinical and molecular information for a case, including multiple samples from the same patient.
Onco Query Language (OQL) More precise queries, specifying mutation classes, exact variants, copy-number states or expression thresholds.
Download and sharing Export tables and results for analysis in R or Python, and save or share queries. Depends on the user's permissions.

4. Use cases#

The portal is especially useful for:

  • Exploring the frequency and distribution of somatic alterations in selected genes in the Brazilian cohorts.
  • Comparing molecular patterns between clinical subgroups, such as subtype, staging or histology.
  • Investigating co-occurring or mutually exclusive alterations that suggest shared biological pathways.
  • Generating hypotheses about biomarkers and molecular signatures for future validation.
  • Checking, before an external analysis, whether a cohort has the required molecular profiles.
  • Reviewing individual cases in their molecular and clinical context.
  • Preparing figures and summaries for meetings, reports and publication planning.

5. How data reach the portal#

Studies are prepared and loaded exclusively by the ATPBR data team. Pipeline results and registered metadata are harmonized, converted to the formats required by cBioPortal and validated before loading. When new assays or corrections become available, the study is reviewed, revalidated and reloaded.

Each study in the portal is made up of the following groups of files:

File group Role in the portal
Study metadata Study identifier, name, description, citation and visibility.
Patient clinical data Patient-level attributes (Table 1).
Sample clinical data Sample-level attributes (Table 2).
Mutation profile Somatic mutations.
Expression profile mRNA and transcript expression.
Copy number Segmented copy-number data.
Case lists Samples available for each type of analysis.

Table 1. Patient-level fields.

Attribute Type Description
PATIENT_ID Text Patient identifier, which links the patient's clinical data to the samples and molecular data.
SAMPLES_PER_PATIENT Number Number of samples from the patient in the study.
SEX Text Sex of the patient.
RACE Text Race/color of the patient, in grouped categories.
COUNTRY Text Country associated with the patient, as defined by the study.
DIAGNOSIS_AGE Number Age at diagnosis, in 5-year bands.
OS_STATUS Text Overall survival status (living or deceased).
OS_MONTHS Number Overall survival time in rounded months, from diagnosis.

Table 2. Sample-level fields.

Attribute Type Description
PATIENT_ID Text Patient to whom the sample belongs.
SAMPLE_ID Text Sample identifier, which links it to the molecular data.
CANCER_TYPE Text Cancer type.
CANCER_TYPE_DETAILED Text Detailed cancer type.
ONCOTREE_CODE Text OncoTree code of the disease.
SAMPLE_TYPE Text Specimen type, such as primary tumor or metastasis.
TUMOR_STAGE_2009 Text Tumor stage.
TREATMENT_DRUG_NAME Text Drug or treatment regimen, when available.
TREATMENT_RESPONSE Text Response to treatment.
WGS_DATA Text Indicates whether the sample has whole genome sequencing (WGS) data.
WES_DATA Text Indicates whether the sample has whole exome sequencing (WES) data.
PANEL_DATA Text Indicates whether the sample has gene panel data.
RNASEQ_DATA Text Indicates whether the sample has RNA-seq data.
METHYLATION_DATA Text Indicates whether the sample has methylome data.

6. Privacy#

All data in the portal are de-identified before release, in line with the principles presented in About ATPBR. De-identified does not mean anonymous: that is why only studies validated and approved for release are published, after an assessment of re-identification risks (see Data governance). Use of the data obtained from the portal, including the prohibition of re-identification attempts and the citation rules, is governed by the Usage policies. Results should be interpreted taking into account the scope and limitations of each cohort.
